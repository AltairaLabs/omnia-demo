# Care Instructions

Acme Apparel uses natural fibers (linen, cotton, wool, cashmere) in most of our pieces. Natural fibers last longer and look better than synthetics, but they need a bit more care. Here's how to keep your pieces looking great for years.

## Always check the care label first

Every garment has a care label sewn in at the inside back neck or side seam. The label is the authoritative guide for that specific piece — these general guidelines apply to most garments in a category but always defer to the label.

## Linen

Linen is one of our favorite fabrics because it gets better with age. It also wrinkles — that's the nature of the fiber, not a defect. Embrace it.

- **Washing**: machine wash cold on the delicate cycle, or hand wash
- **Drying**: lay flat or hang to dry. Tumble drying is OK on low but will increase wear over time and can cause shrinkage
- **Ironing**: iron while slightly damp for best results, medium heat with steam
- **Storage**: hang or fold loosely; linen doesn't need to be stored flat

Linen loves to be worn. The more you wear it, the softer it gets and the better the drape. It's also one of the most durable natural fibers — linen garments routinely last 10+ years with good care.

## Cotton

Standard cotton care works for most Acme cotton pieces.

- **Washing**: machine wash warm or cold, normal cycle
- **Drying**: tumble dry low, or hang to dry to extend garment life
- **Ironing**: medium-high heat with steam
- **Shrinkage**: expect 2-4% shrinkage on first wash for untreated cotton. We pre-wash most cotton pieces during production, so additional shrinkage should be minimal.

**Dark colors** (black, navy, charcoal): wash with like colors the first 2-3 times. The excess dye will rinse out eventually.

## Denim

Our denim is not pre-washed heavily, which means it breaks in to your body over time.

- **First few weeks**: wear as much as possible without washing to let it break in to your shape
- **Washing**: turn inside out, machine wash cold on delicate, wash as infrequently as you can tolerate (seriously — denim does not need to be washed every wear)
- **Drying**: hang dry, or tumble dry on low if you must
- **Stiffness**: new denim can feel stiff; it softens with wear and first wash
- **Color**: dark denim will bleed onto light surfaces (seat cushions, white sneakers) for the first couple of weeks. Consider this when choosing where to sit.

Denim can last decades with proper care. The most common ways people ruin denim: washing it too often (accelerates wear) and washing it in hot water (causes fading and shrinkage).

## Wool and cashmere

Wool and cashmere are delicate and worth the extra care.

- **Washing**: hand wash in cold water with a wool-specific detergent (Woolite or similar). Machine wash is NOT recommended even on the wool cycle — it's one of the top reasons people return wool items saying "it shrunk."
- **Rinsing**: rinse gently without wringing
- **Drying**: lay flat on a towel, roll to press out excess water, reshape, and lay flat to dry. **Never hang wool to dry** — it will stretch out of shape.
- **Storage**: fold and store flat, ideally with cedar blocks or lavender sachets to deter moths
- **Pilling**: wool and cashmere will pill in high-friction areas (underarms, between the arms and body). This is normal. Use a fabric shaver or sweater stone to remove pills — don't pull them off with your fingers, which can damage the fabric.

## Leather (bags and shoes)

- **Cleaning**: wipe with a damp cloth. For deeper cleaning, use a leather-specific cleaner sparingly.
- **Conditioning**: leather needs to be conditioned every 3-6 months to prevent cracking. We recommend Leather Milk or Lexol.
- **Water**: leather is not waterproof. If your bag or shoes get soaked, let them air dry slowly away from direct heat. Direct heat (radiators, hair dryers) will crack the leather.
- **Storage**: stuff bags with tissue paper when not in use to keep their shape. Store shoes with cedar shoe trees.

## General tips

1. **Wash clothes less**. Almost everything can be worn 2-5 times between washes unless it's obviously dirty. Washing is the #1 cause of garment wear.
2. **Cold water is your friend.** Most modern detergents work fine in cold water. Hot water fades colors, damages fibers, and shrinks natural materials.
3. **Mesh bags for delicates.** If you're machine washing anything delicate, put it in a mesh laundry bag to prevent snagging.
4. **Air drying > machine drying**. The tumble dryer is the single most damaging step in the laundry cycle. When you can air dry, do.
5. **If in doubt, hand wash.** Hand washing takes 5 minutes and is almost impossible to mess up.

## When something gets ruined

If you machine-washed something that shouldn't have been machine-washed and it's now the wrong size, we sympathize — but we can't accept returns on customer care errors. Prevention is the best policy. When in doubt, hand wash.

If a garment **fails due to a manufacturing defect** — the seam splits, the buttons all fall off, the fabric pills in ways that seem extreme for normal wear — that's a different story. See `damaged-or-incorrect-items.md`.
